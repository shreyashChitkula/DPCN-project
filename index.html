5 · New dynamical investigations · 2016–2025

Beyond connectivity: can collaboration ties actually carry ideas?

The original dashboard describes the network's structure and its static-network dynamics. These five follow-up experiments ask a harder question: when do those connections provide timely, independent, and useful routes for knowledge to travel? Results below use the complete 2016–2025 years (504 collaborating faculty, 2,192 distinct pairs). They do not replace the 2016–2026 statistics above.

Experiment 01 / Temporal network

How long does a collaboration stay useful?

An idea starts from each of 286 faculty active in 2016 and moves at most one collaboration hop per year. Allowing a tie to remain available for longer changes which faculty are reachable by the end of 2025.

Selected assumptionNo persistence

Reach outside the starting institution

The static graph can overstate timely reach: even one additional year of tie availability changes modeled access substantially. Publication years are proxies for interaction timing, not observed communication dates.

Methodological detail

Each seed is evaluated independently on annual snapshots; reached faculty retain the idea and may transmit in subsequent years. Ties with persistence τ can be used during the paper year and the next τ years. A static 2016–2025 union improperly permits some future edges early and is displayed only as an optimistic benchmark. A shuffle-of-year-order control did not detect an exceptional annual ordering effect at zero memory.

Experiment 02 / Paper-aware null model

Does a coauthor triangle mean a collaboration formed socially?

A single multi-author publication creates a clique of pairwise links. We therefore compare the actual DOI-resolved faculty–paper graph against 120 randomized graphs that retain each paper's year and institutional team sizes and every faculty member's annual paper count.

Observed vs constrained null

The network uses far fewer unique partner pairs and repeats existing partnerships much more often than the paper-aware null predicts. High clustering compared with an Erdős–Rényi graph does not automatically imply unusually strong social triadic closure.

What is held fixed?

Author–paper incidence swaps preserve each faculty member's annual participation and each paper's institutional author counts. Observed and randomized statistics use the same DOI-resolved subset (2,189 observed pairs), not the full collaboration graph used elsewhere in the dashboard. Null bands represent the empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles of the simulations.

Experiment 03 / Independent bridge diversity

Many cross-institute publications—but how many effective relationships?

Count the cross-institution faculty pairs and how frequently each pair appears together. Then compare the diversity of those coauthorship-event weights before and after correcting for very large author teams.

Effective diversity = 1 / Σ(shared event fraction)²

The effective number measures concentration, not observed conversations or literal independent communication channels.

Hyperauthored papers barely change which cross-institution pairs exist, but they dominate raw event weights. Binary connectivity and diversity of repeated interaction are distinct properties.

How to interpret the weighting sensitivity

Raw weights count each coauthored paper equally. The size-adjusted sensitivity discounts each contribution by author-team size; the exclusion sensitivity removes papers with 100 or more authors. Only cross-institution collaborations associated with a DOI-matched paper and known author count are compared across all three conditions.

Experiment 04 / Dynamical spreading

Hearing about an idea is different from adopting a practice

Compare a probabilistic independent cascade, in which one successful exposure can transmit information, with a reinforcement model that requires two or three active collaborators. The two experiments have different starting conditions and are not calibrated to be numerically equivalent.

Simple contagion · one random faculty seed

Each newly reached person tries every collaborator once, with probability T.

Reinforced adoption · group of initial seeds

At least two or three already-adopting neighbors required; starting seeds are a fraction of the chosen institution.

Institutional boundaries and the number of required exposures can sharply constrain modeled adoption, even when a static graph shows short connecting paths. Each model describes a hypothetical diffusion mechanism, not measured uptake of scientific techniques.

Experiment 05 / Network design

What if we could add a handful of cross-institute collaborations?

Choose new candidate edges among recently collaborating faculty and test random, high-degree, long-distance bridge, or greedy structural-efficiency selection. Then validate the selected edges on a separate stochastic diffusion objective.

Change in between-institution shortest-path efficiency

Structural score: institution-pair-balanced inverse-shortest-path efficiency; higher is better.

Independent-cascade reach outside the source institution

Optimizing path efficiency gives substantial structural gains, but no consistent gain in independently simulated cascades. Shorter graph paths do not guarantee more successful information transmission.

Intervention assumptions

Candidate pairs are cross-institution nonedges among recently active faculty with preexisting paths of length 2–4. Each person can receive at most two proposed links. Suggested links are hypothetical network interventions, not evidence of research compatibility or real-world feasibility. Monte Carlo error is displayed in the diffusion comparison; the objective being optimized is structural rather than dynamical.

Method and limits

How this was built

Data and definitions

  • Papers: group30_data.csv (17,328 institute–paper rows, Scopus). Mega paper = ≥100 authors on the source author list.
  • Collaboration network: authorship_network.csv, 9,113 pair-events → 2,356 distinct faculty pairs among 516 faculty (67 more faculty are isolates).
  • Citation network: citation_network.csv, 39,807 unique directed pairs (OpenAlex).
  • Partners: indian_connections.csv. See DECISIONS.md and SOURCES.md for provenance.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Faculty are identified by name in the pair file, so two people with identical names would merge.
  • 191 of 774 roster faculty could not be matched to a unique Scopus ID and are absent from the network.
  • Scopus citation totals are global, not institute-to-institute. Cascade results are simulations on a static network.
  • 2026 is a partial year. Link-prediction AUC ≈ 0.66 means weak signal, not a forecast.
